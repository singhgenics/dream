    Concept 04 · 4.5 × 4.5 × 10.5 m shell · 30 covers

    Dim sum, draught & records

    A warm, rustic pub for dim sum, bar bites, craft beer and cocktails. The bar is the brightest thing in the room. The kitchen is planned the way a commercial kitchen would be, and you can click into it. A loft above the kitchen holds a cushioned snug and the vinyl corner. Every seat has a back. Left and right are as you walk in from the road.

    Loft · 4.5 × 4.5 m over the kitchen and washroom

    • AccessA spiral stair in the second booth's old spot, brass handrail, about 1.4 m across.
    • SeatingA leather banquette along the right wall with back cushions and plaid bolsters, facing floor chairs that have backrests. Two low tables, 4 at each.
    • MusicA vinyl and DJ corner at the back left, with turntables, a mixer and a record wall, looking down over the bar.
    • EdgeA glass balustrade with a timber rail and an LED line under the fascia. The menu board hangs off the front, facing the dining room.
    • HeadroomThe deck sits at 2.55 m, which leaves ~1.95 m up there. That's why the loft has low seating and no bar stools.

    Kitchen · 4.5 × 2.9 m (13 m²)

    • ReceivingA rear service door brings deliveries in and takes waste out, so neither passes through the dining room.
    • Cold storeA 2-door reach-in fridge, plus a freezer under the non-veg prep table.
    • PrepVeg and dumpling-folding prep sits on the front wall, and non-veg prep on the right wall, so raw meat never shares a bench with veg.
    • Hot lineOne 3.4 m baffle-filter hood covers a 2-burner Chinese wok range, a gas dim sum steamer, a twin-basket fryer and a 2-burner range for stock and sauces.
    • PassA plating station under a heat lamp, with a ticket rail. Servers collect through a hatch in the wall, so they never enter the kitchen.
    • DishDirty plates come in by the door and land straight on the dish table: pre-rinse, hood dishwasher, then the 2-bowl pot sink. They never cross the hot line.
    • SafetyA hand-wash sink at the door, a wet-chemical fire suppression system over the line, a K-class extinguisher, an insect killer and a floor drain with a grease trap.
    • Finishes304 stainless steel worktops, white tile to 2.1 m, an epoxy floor with coved edges, and a washable ceiling at 2.4 m (the underside of the loft).
    • ServicesThe LPG cylinders sit outside the back wall in a ventilated cage, with a leak detector and a solenoid shut-off. The exhaust duct leaves the hood straight through the back wall, below the loft, then rises outside past the roof, with an inline fan and a fresh-air make-up fan.

    Kitchens usually take 30–40% of a restaurant's floor. This one takes 28%. That works for dim sum, because dumplings are folded ahead and steamed to order.

    Check before you commit

    • LicenceBar licenceBeer and cocktails need a Goa excise licence. Price it before you build the bar.
    • AccessRear accessThe plan needs a rear door, space for the LPG cage, and a route for the exhaust duct behind the building. Confirm all three with the landlord.
    • NormsOne washroomOne WC is drawn. Check whether the local body asks for separate male and female toilets at 30 covers.
    • SafetyLoft structure and exitA steel deck for 8 people needs a structural engineer. Ask the fire officer whether a spiral stair is acceptable as the only way down.
    • MeasureKitchen ceiling at 2.4 mThat's low for a commercial hood. Have a kitchen consultant confirm the hood and duct sizes fit under the loft.
    • WeatherMonsoon6 of the 30 covers are outside. Plan for 24 covers from June to September.
    • LicenceMusicRecorded music in public usually needs PPL and IPRS (or Novex) licences.
