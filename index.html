    Concept 21 · 4.5 × 4.5 × 10.5 m shell · 32 covers

    Dim sum, draught & records

    A light, warm, cozy pub for cabin-style Chinese, dim sum, bao, Korean bar food, craft beer and cocktails. The ground floor has a cream-and-charcoal checkerboard and brass lanterns. A reclaimed-wood wall carries vintage beer signs, and the bar glows through backlit onyx. Sand limewash, red brick and the raw concrete ceiling keep it bright. Most of the seats are upstairs in an earthy 18-cover loft. Left and right are as you walk in from the road.

    The loft floor is slimmed to 0.17 m (steel joists set within the floor depth, 18 mm ply, thin finish) and sits at 2.38 m. That gives 2.12 m up and 2.2 m below. Over the kitchen hood, a 0.44 m step in the loft floor (under the service station) gives the hood 2.65 m. The National Building Code asks about 2.2 m for a mezzanine and limits it to about a third of the floor area, so have the architect confirm how the local authority will classify the loft.

    Fire & life safety · security

    • StandardLaid out to the National Building Code 2016, Part 4 (Fire & Life Safety), and the usual Goa Fire NOC checklist. Click Safety above to see every device in red. The Goa Directorate of Fire & Emergency Services gives final approval.
    • ExitsFront: glass doors that open outward inside the rolling shutter, with the shutter fully open while trading. Rear: the kitchen door opens outward with a panic bar and is never padlocked during service. The farthest seat is about 18 m from the front door, well inside the 30 m limit.
    • Stair1.2 m clear, 16 risers of 149 mm, 300 mm treads, two flights of 8 with a 1.2 m landing. Wall handrail at 0.9 m, glass balustrade with a 1.0 m rail, and glow anti-slip nosings. It lands through a guarded opening in the loft floor. The under-stair booth went to make room, and the loft's full-height layout won most of those seats back (32 covers).
    • GuardsLoft and nook edges have 1.0 m rails with glass infill in place of horizontal rods, which children could climb.
    • ExtinguishersSeven in total: ABC 6 kg + CO₂ 4.5 kg at the bar, CO₂ at the main electrical board, wet-chemical (Class K) + CO₂ in the kitchen, ABC in the loft and the garden. Mounted with handles at 1–1.5 m and serviced yearly.
    • DetectionSmoke detectors at the front, the stair void, under the loft and in the loft. A heat detector in the kitchen. Manual call points and a hooter at the entrance and the loft stair head, wired to a panel by the electrical board.
    • LightingBattery emergency lights (90 min) at the entrance, the stair landing, the aisle to the kitchen and in the loft. Lit EXIT signs over the front entrance, the loft stair head and the rear door.
    • KitchenWet-chemical hood suppression with a manual pull and an automatic gas cut-off, a fire blanket by the wok range, and a first-aid box. LPG stays outside in a ventilated cage, 76 kg, under the 100 kg limit that triggers a PESO licence.
    • ElectricalThe main board (RCCB + MCBs) and fire alarm panel sit on the right wall past the keg cooler, in the open with clear space in front. Fire-retardant, low-smoke cabling, with a licensed contractor's test certificate.
    • MaterialsUpholstery, rugs, cane, the reclaimed-wood wall, dried flowers and faux plants are flame-retardant treated or certified.
    • Security5 CCTV cameras (entrance, till, loft, rear exit, garden) recording to an NVR in the locked under-stair store. A small safe under the POS. The LPG cage and empty-keg cage are locked.
    • Still to confirmWhether the authority classes the loft as a mezzanine (about 2.2 m height, one-third of floor area) or a floor. Whether a single stair is accepted for 18 loft seats. Whether an accessible toilet is required.

    Loft · 4.5 × 5.7 m · 24.6 m² · 18 covers

    • AccessThe code-compliant stair along the left wall: 1.2 m wide, two gentle flights with a landing, landing through a guarded opening at the front of the loft.
    • SeatingFull-height dining for 18, in an earthy palette. Built-in oak benches run along both walls with rust linen seat cushions, jute back cushions and sage and ochre scatter cushions. They face solid mango-wood tables at 75 cm, with rattan-back dining chairs on the aisle side. Three tables of 4 sit on the right; the left has a table for 4 and a table for 2, with a comfortable gap between them (the stair head stays clear). A jute runner covers the 1.0 m centre aisle, with a woven rattan pendant over each table and a dried-grass bud vase and tea light on every table. The walls are warm clay plaster.
    • Photo spotA standing photo booth in the nook that juts out over the bar. The backdrop is on the nook's right-hand wall, so the booth faces across the room. It has a blush limewash wall, a painted terracotta arch 1.75 m tall, a warm-white "say bao" neon in the arch, a slim brass-edged pill mirror beside it, and a dried-flower garland in dusty pink, rust, wheat and sage cascading down the arch. Guests stand on a jute rug with a brass marker, and the photographer shoots from the nook's open side. The front stays an open black rail carrying the "bao, baby?" neon, so the booth is visible from the door.
    • Service stationThe DJ desk is gone. In its place on the back wall is a loft service station: an olive cabinet with a travertine top and two bus tubs for used plates. It also holds stacked clean plates and bowls, a cutlery caddy, napkins and a bin, so servers don't carry every plate down the stair one at a time. The pink "stay a little longer" neon stays above it.
    • Fake windowsThe loft has no real windows, so it has four fake ones: one on each side wall (1.0 × 0.86 m) and a pair on the back wall (0.8 × 0.8 m) flanking the pink neon. Each is a slim 4000 K LED panel in a shallow box behind a cream cellular blind, cased in painted trim with a sill, on a dimmer that follows the time of day.
    • EdgeA black steel rod railing lined with potted trailing plants, a black fascia with an LED line, and the menu board hanging over the bar.
    • HeadroomThe floor sits at 2.38 m on a slim 0.17 m deck, which leaves 2.12 m up there, enough for adults to stand upright and for normal full-height tables and chairs. Along the back wall the floor steps up 0.44 m to make room for the kitchen hood below. The service station spans that whole step with its front flush to the edge, so the step is just its base and there is no ledge to stand on.

The back bar is sized to the real stock, about 15 bottles, so it looks curated rather than half-empty. A whisky-flight board sits on the counter below. At the door end are two big house-infusion jars with brass taps and three shelves of glassware. At the far end is a brass riser of green soju bottles under a black ON TAP board.
    • LoungeA cane-back sofa with floral cushions seats 3, and a green velvet tub chair adds 1, around a large wooden table on a kilim rug. A vintage brass lantern hangs above. Behind it is a reclaimed-wood plank wall with vintage round and tin beer signs and two Edison sconces.
    • Wall artSand limewash walls in a shade darker than before, so the frames sit into the wall instead of floating on white. About 17 frames in black, oak and brass, each with a cream mat: a gallery over the lounge, a cluster around the billing neon, frames up the stair, a row over each loft banquette and one in the washroom.

    • CoolingTwo 1.4-ton split ACs. The ground-floor unit is high on the kitchen's front wall, blowing down the length of the room. The loft unit is on the loft's back wall, below the pink neon. Both outdoor units hang on brackets on the back wall outside, next to the LPG cage and exhaust.
    • SoundFive wall-mounted speakers angled down at the seats: three downstairs (front right, over the stair, by the server station) and two in the loft.
    • StairA code-compliant stair along the left wall, starting just inside the door: 1.2 m wide, two gentle flights with a landing, oak treads with glow nosings, a brass wall handrail and a glass balustrade. Its solid base hides a locked store, and the bar's extinguishers hang on its side.
    • WashroomOne unisex washroom with one door. Inside are a wall-hung urinal behind a timber modesty screen and a WC, in emerald tile. A shared basin sits right outside the door: a white drum basin under a pop-art face mural whose glasses are two LED ring mirrors.

    Check before you commit

    • LicenceBar licenceBeer and cocktails need a Goa excise licence. Price it before you build the bar.
    • SafetyLoft for 18A steel deck holding 18 people at full tables needs a structural engineer. Ask the fire officer whether one stair is enough, or whether a second way down is required.
    • ServiceFood goes upstairsWith 16 of 30 seats in the loft, most plates climb the stair. Consider a small dumbwaiter from the kitchen pass to the loft.
    • MeasureKitchen ceiling at 2.2 m, 2.65 m over the hoodThe hood sits in a pocket under the stepped-up loft floor. Have a kitchen consultant confirm the hood, duct and fire suppression fit in it.
    • AccessRear accessThe plan needs a rear door, space for the LPG cage, and a route for the exhaust duct behind the building.
    • StructureNook over the barThe photo booth is a standing spot with ~2.12 m clear above the deck, enough for most guests to stand comfortably. The nook cantilevers about 1.25 m past the loft edge over a working bar. The engineer should size it with the loft steel and check that its underside clears the bartender's head at 2.25 m. Fix the mirror and decor securely, since it sits over a working bar.
    • ServicesMist fansThe garden misting needs a water line, a small high-pressure pump, a filter and an outdoor-rated power point. Keep the mist away from the electrics and the billing desk.
    • NormsOne unisex washroomOne room with a WC and a urinal behind a modesty screen, plus a shared basin outside. At 32 covers, check that the local body accepts a single unisex washroom, and whether it must be accessible.
    • SizingTwo 1.4-ton ACs may be too smallThat's 2.8 tons for about 47 m² downstairs plus a 26 m² loft, with a 4.5 m double-height front, a hot kitchen next door, 34 guests and Goa humidity. A common rule for restaurants is roughly 1 ton per 9–10 m², which points to 5–7 tons. Get a heat-load calculation before buying, and consider two 2-ton units or a third unit for the front.
    • WeatherMonsoon6 of the 32 covers are outside. Plan for 26 covers from June to September.
    • LicenceMusicRecorded music in public usually needs PPL and IPRS (or Novex) licences.
