    Concept 11 · 4.5 × 4.5 × 10.5 m shell · 30 covers

    Dim sum, draught & records

    A bright, warm loft-style pub for dim sum, bar bites, craft beer and cocktails. Warm sand limewash walls and red brick, hung with gallery frames, sit under a raw concrete ceiling with a silver spiral duct. Black steel railings frame the loft, and the colours are olive, burnt orange and blush, with warm globe light. Most of the seats are upstairs in a 16-cover loft. A nook that hangs out over the bar is set up as the photo spot. Click the loft or the kitchen to explore them. Left and right are as you walk in from the road.

    Loft · 4.5 × 5.7 m · 16 covers

    • AccessOpen oak treads on a black steel stringer along the left wall, with a slim black handrail. It's safer than a spiral for servers carrying food up to 16 people.
    • SeatingCream bouclé banquettes along both walls with back cushions, facing floor chairs with backrests in olive, orange and blush. Four low tables seat 4 each, with mushroom lamps, a kilim rug and a 1.1 m aisle down the middle. A row of small frames runs above each banquette.
    • Photo spotA standing photo booth in the nook that juts out over the bar. The backdrop is on the nook's right-hand wall, so the booth faces across the room. It has a blush limewash wall, a painted terracotta arch 1.75 m tall, a warm-white "say bao" neon in the arch, a slim brass-edged pill mirror beside it, and a dried-flower garland in dusty pink, rust, wheat and sage cascading down the arch. Guests stand on a jute rug with a brass marker, and the photographer shoots from the nook's open side. The front stays an open black rail carrying the "bao, baby?" neon, so the booth is visible from the door.
    • MusicA vinyl and DJ desk on the back wall, with a record shelf, two speakers and a pink neon above that says "stay a little longer".
    • EdgeA black steel rod railing lined with potted trailing plants, a black fascia with an LED line, and the menu board hanging over the bar.
    • HeadroomThe deck sits at 2.55 m, which leaves ~1.95 m up there. That's why all the loft seating is low.

    Check before you commit

    • LicenceBar licenceBeer and cocktails need a Goa excise licence. Price it before you build the bar.
    • SafetyLoft for 16A steel deck holding 16 people needs a structural engineer. Ask the fire officer whether one stair is enough, or whether a second way down is required.
    • ServiceFood goes upstairsWith 16 of 30 seats in the loft, most plates climb the stair. Consider a small dumbwaiter from the kitchen pass to the loft.
    • MeasureKitchen ceiling at 2.4 mThat's low for a commercial hood. Have a kitchen consultant confirm the hood and duct fit under the loft.
    • AccessRear accessThe plan needs a rear door, space for the LPG cage, and a route for the exhaust duct behind the building.
    • StructureNook over the barThe photo booth is a standing spot with ~1.95 m clear above the deck, which is fine for most guests, but very tall guests should mind their heads. The nook cantilevers about 1.25 m past the loft edge over a working bar. The engineer should size it with the loft steel and check that its underside clears the bartender's head at 2.25 m. Fix the mirror and decor securely, since it sits over a working bar.
    • ServicesMist fansThe garden misting needs a water line, a small high-pressure pump, a filter and an outdoor-rated power point. Keep the mist away from the electrics and the billing desk.
    • NormsUnisex washroomsOne WC cubicle and one urinal cubicle, both unisex, with a shared basin outside. Check that the local body accepts unisex facilities at 30 covers, and whether one cubicle must be accessible.
    • WeatherMonsoon6 of the 30 covers are outside. Plan for 24 covers from June to September.
    • LicenceMusicRecorded music in public usually needs PPL and IPRS (or Novex) licences.
