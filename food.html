Forty choices from about 23 recipes

Your chef's point is right: South Goa families and groups expect a broad Asian menu, and a short one reads as limited. So the menu now offers 40 choices across 25 dishes, but most of the breadth comes from a matrix: one sauce or base, several proteins. Guests see "Chilli · paneer · chicken · pork"; the kitchen sees one recipe. That keeps stock, waste and food cost under control at 12–15 guests a day, and lets one chef and one helper run it.

The cuisine is Pan-Asian with three clear lanes: Indo-Chinese (the volume seller with locals), dim sum and bao (the signature; only The Red Ginger nearby does dim sum), and a short Thai, Burmese and Korean run. No sushi or raw fish: it needs a specialist and a strict cold chain. On the signboard, "Pan-Asian" reads more current than "Oriental".

Running it with one chef and one helper

  • Chef: owns the wok and the fryer during service, tastes every sauce base, and folds dim sum on the two batch days.
  • Helper: veg prep and cutting, rice (two rice cookers: jasmine and sticky), steamer timing, soups and curries from the pre-made bases, plating, garnish and dishes.
  • Two batch days a week (Mon and Thu, 10 am–1 pm): fold all four dim sum and freeze in trays of 4; braise the pork belly; make the curry pastes, chilli oil and gochujang sauce; ferment the week's kimchi. Bao buns are bought in frozen.
  • Every morning (1.5 h): chilli, Manchurian and honey-chilli sauces; batter; cut veg; cook rice for tomorrow's fried rice; hot & sour and tom kha stock.
  • During service almost everything is a 4–6 minute finish: fry and toss, steam from frozen (8–10 min, self-timed), or ladle a base and heat. Two people can comfortably do 35–45 covers a service on this menu.
  • Limits to set: salt & pepper chonak only when the fish is good that day; green curry and khao suey are made from one morning base each, so 86 them when it runs out rather than cooking a second batch at 9 pm.
  • Kit: no new equipment beyond a second rice cooker, a stone mortar for the papaya salad, and ideally one more under-counter fridge for sauces and cut veg.

What changed from the 22-dish menu

  • Added: tom kha, chicken wontons in chilli oil, prawn & chive dumplings, raw papaya salad, Thai basil chicken bowl, jasmine rice, Thai green curry, khao suey, coconut sticky rice, and protein choices on the starters, noodles, rice and gravies.
  • Dropped: the chimney soup for 2–3 (every soup is now one bowl, one person; Wonton Kenobi reuses the wonton batch), har gow and xiao long bao (the most labour per basket for one chef), golden fried prawns as a separate dish (now in Salt & Pepa), and the separate chilli pork and chilli paneer lines (now Cabin Fever choices).
  • Kept as signatures: Cabin Fever, chouriço siu mai, pork belly bao, Korean wings, darsaan.
