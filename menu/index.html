The idea: Kolkata Chinese, done properly, in a Goan pub

Every Goan town has Indo-Chinese, but it's mostly the same generic chilli-and-cornflour cooking. Kolkata's Tangra cabins are the original: high-flame wok hei, double-fried batters, golden fried prawns with kasundi mustard, chilli pork, chimney soup, darsaan with ice cream. That gives you something locals recognise and can tell is better. Dim sum and bao bring the photo moments, and the Korean corner brings the "only one in Betalbatim" draw, with no Korean food within about 6 km. Goan touches are kept to a few: chouriço siu mai, a cafreal bao, kokum soda, a kokum gose and two feni cocktails.

The menu has 39 dishes. That's wide, but the Indo-Chinese runs off five mother sauces and two batters. Cut the bottom 15–20% after the first 6–8 weeks of sales.

Who it's for

  • Locals, 25–40, from Majorda, Colva, Benaulim and Margao: chilli chicken, chowmein and a pint after work.
  • Bengali and North Indian tourists: they know Kolkata Chinese and will travel for golden fried prawns and chimney soup.
  • Groups: the loft, chimney soup for the table, pitchers, the feast set.
  • Families at lunch: 15 veg dishes plus veg versions of the momos, fried rice and tteokbokki, along with combos and zero-proof drinks.

Kitchen stations

  • Wok range (2 burners): all the Indo-Chinese, Korean stir-fries and fried rice. It runs off five mother sauces made each morning: chilli-garlic, Manchurian, hot garlic, schezwan and Kolkata red chilli sauce.
  • Fryer: two batters (egg-cornflour and a lighter tempura). It does chilli chicken, prawns, fish, baby corn, potato, wings and cauliflower first, then they go to the wok to be tossed.
  • Steamer: dim sum, momos, xiao long bao and bao buns.
  • 2-burner range: soups, stocks, tteokbokki, chimney soup base and darsaan syrup.
  • New equipment: 2–3 brass or steel chimney hotpots for table service.

Sourcing

  • Bhetki: use Goa's own chonak (the same fish, barramundi) from Margao market.
  • Kasundi mustard, Kolkata red chilli sauce: make in house, or bring in Kolkata brands.
  • Pork: a Goan butcher for chilli pork and xiao long bao. Chouriço from Margao market.
  • Bao: house-made steamed buns, with a poi-flour blend for the Goa-touch cafreal bao.
  • Gochujang, rice cakes, soju, glass noodles: Indian importers. Keep about a month of stock.
  • Craft beer: rotating kegs from Goa and Indian craft brewers.

Whisky & vodka: small and niche

Keep the list to about ten bottles rather than a wall of big brands. Lead with Indian single malts, starting with Paul John, distilled in Cuncolim about 15 km away. That's a local story worth telling. Add two Japanese pours for the Asian theme and three house-infused vodkas, which cost little to make and match the food. The single-malt flight raises spend per head and gets people talking.

Licence dependency

The taps and cocktails need the Goa excise licence. If it isn't through on opening day, the zero-proof list covers the bar, and the food menu stands on its own. Recorded music needs PPL and IPRS (or Novex) licences.
