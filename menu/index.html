Cabin-style Chinese · dim sum & bao · Korean bar food · craft taps

Indo-Chinese the way the old Chinese cabins of the City of Joy cook it: high flame, double-fried, extra chilli, straight out of Tangra. Alongside it are hand-pleated dim sum, steamed bao, Korean bar food and cold craft beer. A few dishes borrow from the Konkan pantry around us.

Prices in ₹, taxes extra. Dim sum come 4 to a basket, bao 2 to a plate. Spirits are poured in 30 ml / 60 ml. Tell us about allergies: shellfish, pork, peanuts, gluten, egg, soy, sesame. Taps rotate weekly.

Twenty dishes, chosen to win

The food is cut to 20 dishes, from soups to desserts, so the kitchen can nail every one. Each dish was scored on five things: whether it suits a pub and sells with beer, whether it's different from the 26 Asian places within 6 km, whether people will photograph it, whether it can hold a 30% food cost, and whether it shares prep and stations with other dishes.

Within 6 km there are about 10 momo outlets and 8 Chinese restaurants, so generic momos and Indo-Chinese are crowded and cheap. Red Ginger (4.6 from about 4,000 reviews) already does dim sum. Nobody nearby does Korean, bao or the Tangra signatures. Those are the gaps this menu fills.

What was cut (21 dishes), and why

  • Momos and potstickers: the market is flooded (about ₹100 for 7 nearby), and they'd dilute the dim sum.
  • Sweet corn and manchow soups: generic, and two soups are enough.
  • Drums of heaven: overlaps the Korean wings. Keep one wing dish.
  • Garlic pepper fish and fish in hot garlic sauce: about 35–40% food cost, with a volatile supply.
  • Veg Manchurian (dry) and crispy baby corn: found everywhere as starters. Manchurian returns as a veg gravy main instead.
  • Chicken siu mai, chilli chicken bao and cafreal bao: they overlapped other dishes. Four dim sum and two bao is the sweet spot.
  • Korean cauliflower, tteokbokki and japchae: extra imported stock (rice cakes, glass noodles) for slower sellers.
  • Hakka noodles and chilli chicken gravy: folded into chowmein's veg and chicken options, and the combos.
  • Mango sticky rice: seasonal only. It can come back as an April–June special.
  • Kimchi fried rice and date pancakes: cut to make room for two veg mains and stay at 20. The mixed fried rice covers rice, and darsaan covers dessert.

Kitchen load across the four stations

  • Wok (8): chilli chicken, chilli pork, chilli paneer, honey chilli potato (finish), chowmein, fried rice, veg Manchurian gravy, tofu & mushroom hot garlic.
  • Fryer (8): chilli chicken and paneer batters, golden prawns, wings, kimchi fries, honey chilli potato, Manchurian balls, tofu, darsaan.
  • Steamer (6): four dim sum and two bao.
  • Range (3): hot & sour, the chimney soup base, the darsaan honey.
  • Shared prep: one chilli-garlic base, one gochujang sauce, one kimchi batch and one egg-cornflour batter cover most of the menu.

Whisky & vodka: small and niche

Keep the list to about ten bottles. Lead with Indian single malts, starting with Paul John, distilled in Cuncolim about 15 km away. Add two Japanese pours for the Asian theme and two house-infused vodkas, which cost little and match the food.

Spirit prices are indicative. Check them against current Goa excise MRPs before printing.

Licences

The taps and cocktails need the Goa excise licence. If it isn't through on opening day, the zero-proof list covers the bar. Recorded music needs PPL and IPRS (or Novex) licences.
